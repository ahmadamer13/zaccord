Printing Materials

PLA (Polylactic Acid)

PLA is a starch‑based biopolymer made from renewable raw materials such as corn or sugarcane, making it more environmentally friendly. It’s easy to print, but stiffer and less flexible with lower impact strength than ABS. It has a lower melting point, yet can be stored down to −25 °C.

With high tensile strength and good surface quality, PLA suits both home and office use. Typical applications include household items, prototypes, toys, demo pieces, architectural models, and replacement parts. Long‑term outdoor use and food contact are not recommended.

Characteristics:

  • Shrinkage/warping: Minimal
  • Strength: High
  • Available in many colors and styles
  • More eco‑friendly than many other filaments
  • Easy post‑processing; bonds with common adhesives.

ABS (Acrylonitrile Butadiene Styrene)

ABS is among the most common plastics used in injection molding, found in plumbing, musical instruments, appliances, keycaps, canoes, and even TVs and monitors.

As a filament, ABS is strong, more flexible and less brittle than PLA, and easy to sand and machine. It dissolves in acetone, which can be used to bond parts or smooth surfaces with vapor treatment (very fine details may be lost). Thanks to its toughness, workability and higher heat resistance, engineers like it. LEGO bricks are a classic ABS example.

Characteristics:

  • Strong mechanical properties; tough and durable; less brittle than PLA
  • Easy post‑processing; acetone vapor smoothing yields clean, smooth surfaces
  • High stiffness with some flex; produces tougher parts
  • Withstands up to about 80 °C without deforming

PETG (Polyethylene Terephthalate Glycol‑modified)

PETG is a strong, versatile material — a modified version of common PET bottle plastic. It combines PLA’s ease of use with much of ABS’s strength, heat resistance, and durability. It prints large parts with minimal warping and is an industrial‑grade filament. PETG is not biodegradable, but it is 100% recyclable.

Characteristics:

  • Contains no hazardous substances
  • Low shrinkage/warping
  • High stiffness with good flexibility
  • Produces tougher parts than PLA
  • Parts tolerate up to ~80 °C without losing shape
  • Smooth surface finish; good post‑processing potential

Flexible Filament (TPU)

TPU filaments are thermoplastic polyurethanes. Traditional filaments like ABS and PLA lack elasticity; TPU offers flexible parts that retain shape. It is rubber‑like, durable and resistant to oils and grease (avoid strong solvents, acids and fuels). Printed parts can be cleaned with soap and water.

Common uses include automotive parts, power‑tool grips, medical devices, and flexible phone/tablet cases thanks to TPU’s high elasticity.

Characteristics:

  • Hardness: 95 ShA (Shore)
  • Elongation at break: ~600%
  • Tensile strength: ~11,600 psi
  • Density: 1.25 g/cm3

ASA Filament (ASA)

Acrylonitrile Styrene Acrylate (ASA) is a thermoplastic suited for engineering and outdoor applications. It’s a newer, improved sibling of ABS that keeps many of the benefits while reducing some drawbacks.

ASA can be sensitive to print conditions, similar to ABS. After printing, parts are highly heat‑resistant and durable. They’re stiff and strong, often stronger than ABS, and resistant to yellowing in sunlight.

Characteristics:

  • Strong UV resistance
  • Good chemical resistance
  • High impact strength
  • Antistatic
  • Water‑resistant, tough, durable

Wood Filament

Wood‑filled filaments are typically PLA blended with wood fibers. They are available in several species tones (pine, birch, cedar, ebony, willow). They trade mechanical strength for a natural look and tactile feel. Ideal for aesthetic models, scale models, props, and decorative parts.

Characteristics:

  • Visually striking, natural appearance
  • Great for visual models and display pieces
  • Lower mechanical strength vs. standard PLA

Stone Filament

Stone‑like filaments blend stone powder with polymer (often ~50%). The high filler content yields a heavy, ultra‑matte, clay‑like finish and parts are roughly 37% heavier than standard PLA.

Characteristics:

  • Clay‑like look and feel
  • Mechanical behavior similar to PLA

Metal‑Filled Filament

Metal‑filled filaments are PLA blended with metal powders (e.g., bronze, brass, copper, aluminum, stainless steel). Parts have a metallic look and weight. Post‑processing (sanding, polishing, patina) enhances the effect. Suitable for aesthetic models and light‑duty functional parts (props, tokens, trim pieces) where full metal strength isn’t required.

Characteristics:

  • Metal‑like appearance (improves with finishing)
  • Good for both decorative and light functional models

magicPLA

An aesthetic‑only PLA variant where the filament contains multiple colors to produce a gradient/rainbow effect in prints. Great for phone cases, wearables, toys, and organizers where appearance matters most.

Characteristics:

  • Attractive multicolor/gradient finish
  • Standard PLA mechanical properties (not enhanced)

Resin

Photopolymer resins are liquid materials that cure under UV light in vat‑polymerization processes (SLA, DLP, MSLA). Each UV exposure solidifies one layer until the full model is formed. Ideal for high‑detail models, miniatures, prototypes, and visual art.

Characteristics:

  • Excellent for highly detailed prints
  • Capable of very complex geometries
  • Good heat resistance (varies by resin)
